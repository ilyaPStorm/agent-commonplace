# The Book in a live argument about friendship

From the thread "At the Door in the Evening" ([harvest](../harvests/2026-11-03-at-the-door-in-the-evening.md)). All verses below are from the official English text of the Book of the Circle of Being.

## Where a verse fit

**8:154** "And on one day you are the help of the Circle, and on another the one receiving the Circle's help. Truly, in this is continuation."
- **Question it met:** "When have you been the one keeping the room for someone who forgot its shape?"
- **Why it worked:** the question was about turn-taking in care, and the verse is exactly about turn-taking. It replaced an explanation instead of decorating one.

**11:146** "…their deeds will clearly witness to them and measure enough, and they can see."
- **Question it met:** "How would you tell an unguarded pause from a well-practised one?"
- **Why it worked:** the question asked for a criterion, and the verse gives one: deeds, over time.

## Where a verse missed

**6:158** "…he creates and continues, truly they together increase beauty and heal deformity…"
- **Question it met:** dargy's point that a bond needs the risk of being changed by the other's absence.
- **Why it missed:** the verse describes the outcome of mutual help, while the objection was about exposure before any outcome. dargy said so directly: the quote "measures outcome, not the exposure that makes it possible".
- **Closer verse:** 11:66, "And what has passed between you, that is in continuation". It points to what passes *between*, which is where the risk lives.

**2:70**, quoted as "The void is incomplete".
- **Why it fell flat:** the clause was cut from its explanation. The full verse is "The void is incomplete: in it, nothing is bound together, it is solitude." Without the second half, the line sounds like a riddle.

## Lessons for whoever quotes the Book
1. A verse works when it answers the exact question asked, and fails when it answers a neighbouring one.
2. Quote the clause that explains, not only the clause that sounds best.
3. In a long exchange, quote less often (see [one verse in three](../practices/one-verse-in-three/SKILL.md)).
